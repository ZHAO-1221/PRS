# iOS Playful Pet-Care Direction Study

## Product and context

This is an English-language iOS prototype for a single, focused function: a dog owner uploads a relatively long home video (typically around 30 minutes), the system detects dog activities and human–dog interactions, and the owner receives a time-based summary. Each detected segment can be opened and played as a corresponding clip. There is currently no product name, logo, final brand system, or real user video. The existing generated indoor frame is retained as honest demo content. The study does not redesign the underlying information architecture yet; it explores how the same feature can feel more lively, memorable, and pet-specific.

## Audience and situation

The primary user is an everyday dog owner, not a researcher or professional trainer. They may review the result casually after work, so the interface must feel warm and rewarding while keeping timestamps and activity labels immediately readable. The app should communicate that the AI has done useful work without looking clinical, surveillance-heavy, childish, or like a pet e-commerce store. The emotional target is “delightful reassurance”: the owner should feel they have received a small story about their dog’s day, not merely a machine-generated detection table.

## Required content

- A completed-analysis state for a 30-minute recording from 09:00 to 09:30.
- A visible video frame and a direct way to play the selected clip.
- Dog behavior and human–dog interaction represented as separate semantic dimensions.
- At least one selected event: “Eating while being fed”, 09:08–09:10.
- Summary values: Rest 19 min, Active 11 min, Together 5 min.
- English-only interface copy.
- Pet-related elements must support function or brand recognition. Decorative paw prints may appear only as a restrained pattern or progress cue; they must not replace labels.

## Visual and interaction goals

The three directions must use materially different layout grammars, not identical screens with palette swaps. Direction 1 interprets moments as tactile photo stickers and a scrapbook story. Direction 2 uses a curved “tail trail” as the timeline and makes the dog profile the emotional anchor. Direction 3 borrows the bright, solid, chew-toy quality of playful pet branding and organizes information into chunky modular blocks. All directions retain iOS legibility, large touch targets, semantic color roles, and a clear hierarchy. The preview canvas is designed for side-by-side comparison; each individual device uses the standard iPhone 15 Pro frame at 393 × 852 logical pixels.

## Image strategy

The video frame is content-essential because the product’s value is reviewing actual detected moments. It is therefore reused in all three directions. No unrelated stock lifestyle photography is added. The pet motifs are lightweight graphic forms derived from the product’s content—paw steps, a tail-like path, collar-tag tabs, bone/treat shapes—and are not presented as literal scientific diagrams or proprietary brand assets.

## Research anchors

- Apple’s 2025 Design Award coverage of CapWords: tactile sticker metaphors, real-world physics, warmth, and micro-feedback can make an AI process feel humane and delightful.
- Tractive: pet activity products must preserve clear summaries and interpretable time/activity information.
- Woofz: dog-centric tone and positive reinforcement demonstrate that credible pet tools can still feel friendly.
- Healthy Paws brand guidance: organic rounded shapes can create approachable rhythm without playfulness for its own sake.

## Assumptions

- “Milo” is a provisional dog name used only for realistic content.
- “Activity Review” is a functional heading, not the future product name.
- The direction study is static; after the user selects a direction, the chosen system will be expanded into 4–6 interactive iOS screens.
- Red remains reserved for errors or urgent alerts in the future product; current interaction accents use coral, orange, yellow, blue, or green instead.
